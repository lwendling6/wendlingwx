What this is

You text a short request from your inReach. A free service pulls a high resolution weather model for your exact spot, packs a full forecast into one to three messages of coded letters, and sends them back. This app builds the request and reads the reply, entirely offline.

Which source to pick

HRRR-AK 48H. The highest resolution model available for Alaska, on a 3 km grid, so it actually resolves terrain: inlet channeling, gap winds, the difference between a valley and the ridge above it. Best for anything inside two days, and the only source with winds aloft.

Blend 5-day. HRRR-AK for the first 48 hours, then a global model for days 3 to 5 at roughly 11 to 13 km. Fine for "is a system coming", poor for timing. Planning, not commitment.

NWS 5-day. National Weather Service grids, model output adjusted by Alaska forecasters who know the local biases. Different failure modes from raw model output, so when it and HRRR-AK agree your confidence should go up. That is why both are here.

Which reply detail to pick

Every step carries the same fields. Length buys time resolution, not more information per step. Each message counts against your inReach plan, so Basic is the cheap habit.

Basic is HRRR-AK only. One message cannot carry five days at any resolution worth having, so the other two sources grey out.

Winds aloft come only from HRRR-AK, and only within 48 hours. On Standard and Full you get three levels: 3,000, 6,000 and 10,000 ft above sea level. Basic squeezes to a single 5,000 ft level, because that is what fits in one message. Neither NWS nor days 3 to 5 of the blend carry winds aloft, because those sources do not provide them.

Before every trip, in town

  1. Open this app in airplane mode. If it does not load, open it once with signal and try again. Do not leave until it loads offline.
  2. Save the wx address as a contact on the inReach.
  3. Send one test request from the trailhead and decode the reply, so you know the loop is alive before you need it.

Requesting a forecast

  1. Pick the reply detail and the source.
  2. Tap Use current location. Step into the open if it fails. GPS works in airplane mode.
  3. Tap Copy message and send it to your wx address.
  4. Leave location sharing on if you can. The service then uses the inReach's own coordinates, which beat a phone fix under trees. With sharing off, the coordinates in the message text are used.
  5. Wait. Ten to twenty minutes is normal.

Reading the reply

  1. Copy each reply message.
  2. Open Decode and paste each into its own box. Order does not matter. Add boxes if you asked for more than two.
  3. Tap Read forecast.

If it says a part is missing, one message has not arrived yet. If the checksum failed, a message was garbled or truncated: re-copy each in full and try again.

Reading the chart

The plot at the top is temperature across the whole period, so you see the shape before reading any numbers. Below it the forecast is a table you scroll sideways: time runs left to right, variables run down. The first column stays put while you scroll, so you always know which row you are reading. Wind arrows point the way the wind is travelling; the compass letters say where it comes from.

An amber vertical line marks a change in the forecast behind the chart. Sometimes that is a change of time step, for example Full switching from hourly to 2 hourly after the first day, so the columns to its right cover twice as long. Sometimes it is a change of data source, for example HRRR-AK handing off to the global model on day 3. Either way, read the columns to the right of it as coarser, and in the 5 day options, less trustworthy.

If things go wrong

  1. No reply after 30 minutes: resend. Satellite messages do go missing. A reply saying the forecast failed means try again in 15 minutes.
  2. Reply is readable text, not code: the service hit an error and is telling you what happened.
  3. Phone dead: the coded reply is unreadable without this app. Treat this as a supplement, never the plan.
This is a hobby service with no guarantee. It depends on free tiers of several services and a weather model that is wrong sometimes, especially in valleys during inversions. Never bet a river crossing, a flight, or an exposed night on the ridge on a single forecast from anything, including this.
