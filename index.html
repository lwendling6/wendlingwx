WendlingWx / field forecast

What this is

You send a short message over your satellite device. A free service pulls a high resolution weather model for your exact spot, squeezes a full forecast into a few text messages of coded letters, and sends it back. This app builds the request and reads the reply. It works entirely offline once installed.

Before every trip, in town

  1. Open this app in airplane mode. If it does not load, open it once with signal and test again. Do not leave until it loads offline.
  2. Confirm the Send to address is filled in on the Request tab. It is saved on the phone, per channel.
  3. inReach: save the wx address as a contact, on the device or in the Garmin app, whichever you send from.
  4. iPhone satellite: exchange one message with the wx number while you still have coverage. Apple can refuse satellite messages to contacts you have not messaged recently.
  5. Send one test request from the trailhead and decode the reply, so you know the whole loop is alive before you need it.

Requesting a forecast in the field

  1. Pick your channel. inReach replies fit in 2 messages. iPhone sat text replies use 4, with room for more detail.
  2. Pick the forecast. 48 h HRRR-AK is the sharpest short range model for Alaska terrain. 5 day keeps HRRR for the first two days and adds a coarser global model beyond. 5 day NWS uses the forecaster-adjusted National Weather Service grids instead.
  3. Tap Use my location. Step into the open if it fails. GPS works fine in airplane mode.
  4. Tap Copy message, paste it into a new message to your wx address, and send. The coordinates travel inside the message text, so it works from the inReach itself or from the Garmin app.
  5. Wait. Ten to twenty minutes is normal. The reply arrives as 2 to 4 messages of coded letters.

Reading the reply

  1. Copy each reply message. On the inReach, read them off the screen into the box if pairing is being difficult.
  2. Open the Decode tab and paste all of them into the box. Any order. Line breaks, spaces, or all of them run together in one blob all work.
  3. Tap Read forecast.

If it says a part is missing, one message has not arrived yet or you missed one when copying. If it says the checksum failed, a message got garbled or cut off: re-copy each one completely and try again.

Reading the display

Each row is one time step. The dot is temperature, positioned cold to warm across the row, with the exact value inside it. Below each row the wind is spelled out, like "from the west-southwest at 18 mph, gusts to 31," and any precipitation is named, like "0.12 in rain" or "0.40 in snow." The bar under the dot shows precipitation amount at a glance, blue for rain and indigo for snow. The banner at the top compares the freezing level to your elevation; red means it reaches you. Ridge winds, when present, are the 850 and 700 mb winds, roughly 5,000 and 10,000 ft.

If things go wrong

  1. No reply after 30 minutes: resend. Satellite messages do go missing. If a reply says the forecast failed, wait 15 minutes and try again.
  2. Reply is readable text, not code: the backend hit an error and is telling you what happened.
  3. Phone dead or drowned: the coded reply is unreadable without this app. Carry paper knowledge of the synoptic pattern before you leave, and treat this as a supplement, never the plan.
This is a hobby service with no guarantee. It depends on free tiers of several services and a weather model that is wrong sometimes, especially in valleys during inversions. Never bet a river crossing, a flight, or an exposed ridge on a single forecast from anything, including this.
